# ADR-0012 — Geometry first, artwork second for interactive 3D worlds

**Status:** Accepted, mandatory  
**Decision date:** 31 October 2026  
**Scope:** DaveHub's interactive 3D worlds, furniture, mechanisms, camera transitions, and any future scene with perspective-dependent moving parts.  
**Owner approval:** Explicit instruction to record this lesson permanently after successful DH-11C.1 and DH-11C.2 geometry-first prototypes.

## The rule, verbatim

> **Do not go back to “generate a bunch of pictures and try to fake a 3D world.”**  
> **Build the world properly, then make it beautiful.**

This is an **architecture constraint**, not merely a visual preference.

## Context: the failure we must not repeat

The Manuscript Hoard filing cabinet was initially assembled from independently AI-generated cabinet and drawer images. The closed cabinet, extended drawers and interiors had inconsistent dimensions, camera positions, vanishing points, lighting and occlusion. Attempting to patch them together into one working object repeatedly made the perspective visibly wrong. Generating more variants did not solve the structural mismatch.

The successful reset was to establish the cabinet's dimensions, shared coordinate system, camera and drawer travel as real geometry before attempting an illustrated finish. DH-11C.1 proved drawer motion with CSS 3D; DH-11C.2 extended this approach to a single Three.js room with objects, one camera and coherent interactions.

## Decision

1. **Model geometry before generating visual finishes.** Establish scale, axes, pivots, depth, object hierarchy, occlusion, camera framing and physical interaction first. Demonstrate working mechanisms with untextured or simple-material geometry.
2. **Keep one geometric source of truth.** All moving parts of an interactive object must be rigged or constructed relative to the same model and perspective. Drawers slide on their defined axes; doors rotate around real hinges; papers, folders, keys and other manipulable parts have consistent transforms.
3. **For substantial rooms, use a proper 3D asset workflow.** Use Blender or an equivalent modelling tool for detailed assets when warranted; export GLB/glTF with correct pivots, UVs and material slots; render them through the existing scene architecture (currently Three.js). Procedural geometry is entirely valid for prototypes or suitable final objects.
4. **Art is applied to verified geometry.** Painterly materials, approved colour palettes, decals, PBR textures, lighting, shaders, baked detail and atmospheric effects may enhance a working scene. AI-generated images may inform art direction or supply suitable surface/decal concepts. They must never be used as independent, perspective-critical substitutes for separate geometric states.
5. **Animations must be driven by real part movement.** Animate model bones, pivots, transforms or one controlled source scene. If pre-rendered 2D frames are preferred for performance, render every state from that **same model, fixed camera, lighting and rig**. Never create opening/closing frames independently and attempt to align them afterwards.
6. **Scene-first interaction.** Maintain one shared coordinate system and curated cameras for a room. Make furniture and world objects the interaction surfaces rather than wrapping ordinary content cards in illustrations.
7. **Review mechanics before polish.** Require a demonstrable geometry/movement prototype and owner visual approval before extensive texturing, decoration or content integration. Validate at least closed, mid-transition, fully open, reverse transition, and representative camera angles.
8. **Maintain existing boundaries.** Keep accessible HTML alternatives, touch and keyboard controls, reduced-motion support, safe/public catalogue isolation, Cloudflare-first staging verification and explicit approval before replacing published experiences.

## Forbidden implementation patterns

- Building a functioning three-dimensional scene by independently image-generating a background, closed state, open state and moving fragments.
- Faking depth by overlaying arbitrary images of objects whose perspective or silhouette changes between generated states.
- Repairing inconsistent vanishing points with scaling, skewing, masking or further AI-generated patchwork rather than correcting geometry.
- Treating a polished static illustration as proof that an object can open, rotate, extend, be inspected or be reused from another camera angle.
- Integrating an image-based mechanism into a public world before demonstrating physical motion in a hosted prototype.

## Permitted uses of images

This does **not** prohibit original illustration, generated concept art, static decorative imagery, paintings in the room, signage, skyboxes, textures, UI art or 2D-only experiences. It specifically prohibits using unrelated pictures as the mechanical truth of an interactive 3D scene. A 2D presentation rendered **consistently from a single 3D model** is also acceptable.

## Required workflow for future DH-11 worlds

1. Define the object map, geometry, shared camera, interactive hierarchy and state transitions.
2. Build a playable untextured geometry prototype in a separate staging preview.
3. Test depth, contact, occlusion, animation, camera movement, responsive framing and accessibility; obtain owner visual approval.
4. Build or refine reusable 3D models with independent movable components; use GLB/glTF as appropriate.
5. Apply approved stylised textures, colour, lighting, effects and decorative images **to that geometry**.
6. Bind approved catalogue content and verify that no unpublished items appear.
7. Run regression and hosted Cloudflare checks, then obtain explicit owner approval before promoting the scene into the real world page.

## Exceptions and change control

A future proposal that deviates from this rule must explicitly explain its geometry/perspective guarantees and obtain a **new, owner-approved ADR superseding ADR-0012** before implementation. Do not silently revert because image generation seems faster or prettier.
